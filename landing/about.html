About ClassTrack AI

Calling out names or passing around a sign-in sheet eats into every class, and it is easy to get wrong. ClassTrack AI gives that time back: the teacher takes one photo of the room, and the AI works out who is there.

What it does

Face recognition attendance

Upload or capture classroom photos. Every recognized, enrolled student is marked present.

Voice attendance

Record students saying "present". Students who enrolled their voice are recognized by it.

Join with a code or QR

Teachers share a subject code, link, or QR code. Students enroll in as many subjects as they need.

Teacher stays in control

Results are reviewed before saving, can be corrected later, and can be downloaded as a spreadsheet.

How the AI works

Faces. When a student signs up, ClassTrack AI finds their face in the webcam photo and turns it into 128 numbers that describe its shape (a "face embedding"), using the open-source dlib face recognition model. In a classroom photo, every face is converted the same way and compared with the enrolled students. A student is only marked present when the match is close enough, so look-alikes and strangers are left out.

Voices. Students can optionally record a short phrase. It is turned into a "voiceprint" with the open-source Resemblyzer model, and speakers in a classroom recording are matched against those voiceprints.

People check the result. The AI suggests who is present; the teacher confirms before anything is saved. Recognition works best with good lighting and faces turned towards the camera.

Privacy by design

  • Students agree to the Privacy Policy before their face or voice data is stored.
  • Photos and recordings are processed and then discarded. Only the numeric embeddings are kept.
  • Teacher passwords are stored as one-way bcrypt hashes, never in plain text.
  • The database is locked down so only the app's server, using a private key, can read or write data.

Open source

ClassTrack AI is built with Python, Streamlit, Supabase (PostgreSQL), dlib, scikit-learn and Resemblyzer. The full source code is on GitHub.
